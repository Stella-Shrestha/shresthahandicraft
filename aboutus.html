40+ Year Newar Heritage Atelier

The Sacred Lineage of Patan Master Sculptors

Patan (Lalitpur), the ancient City of Fine Arts, has preserved the lost-wax casting philosophy (Cire Perdue) since the Licchavi and Malla dynasties. Shrestha Handicraft stands as one of the last dedicated sacred ateliers upholding uninterrupted adherence to Sanskrit iconometric treatises.

Founded by the Tamrakar clan in the historic artisan quarters of Patan over four decades ago, Shrestha Handicraft was established with a singular devotion: to preserve the living ritual tradition of Newar sacred metallurgy against the tide of commercial mass production.

"Without correct measurement, the deity cannot abide in the image. Iconometry is the breath of the bronze."
— Samarangana Sutradhara, Sloka 108

Unlike modern synthetic resin molding, every statue commissioned in our Patan foundry begins with hand-carved beeswax blended with Himalayan sal tree resin. Once melted away in the crucible furnace at 1,080°C, the void receives the molten Ashtadhatu 8-metal alloy — creating a singular, unrepeatable masterpiece imbued with devotional spirit.

Over four decades, our sculptures have been consecrated in revered Buddhist monasteries across Bhutan, Tibet, Ladakh, Japan, and Thailand, as well as treasured by premier museums and private sacred art collectors in 34 nations.
